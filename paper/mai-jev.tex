\title{\textbf{mai-jev}: A Calibrated, Quantised, 322M Decision Model for Chinese Customer-Service Ticket Triage}
\author{Maicarons Mai-jev Contributors}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{mai-jev}, a Chinese-first System~One decision model for customer-service ticket triage that answers three decision primitives in a single forward pass: which team should own a ticket (\texttt{choice}), how urgent it is (\texttt{score}), and whether the customer is asking for a refund, compensation or escalation (\texttt{noul}). Starting from the 322M-parameter multilingual checkpoint of Laya~\cite{laya}, we RLCD-fine-tune on \DataTotalRows{} audited Chinese rows, temperature-calibrate on a disjoint split to a worst-case expected calibration error of \WorstEce{} --- marginally above our \num{0.10} target, which we report as a miss rather than tune away --- and ship a single-graph int8-gather ONNX package of \ModelMb\,MB that answers all three primitives for one ticket within \RssMb\,MB resident memory on four CPU threads, at a p50 latency of \LatPFiveZeroMedian\,ms over \LatSessions{} sessions of the same binary on one host --- a host-side span of \LatPFiveZeroBest--\LatPFiveZeroWorst\,ms, larger than every model-side effect we measure.

Two findings are negative and, we argue, more reusable than the headline numbers. First, proxy labels are the dominant error source, and we hit the same trap twice. An earlier checkpoint reached only \AccScoreVZeroTwo{} on urgency purely because the public proxy annotated \emph{emotion intensity}, not ticket urgency; replacing those labels with evidence-graded ones moved the same primitive to \AccScore{} (\ie\ a swing of \DeltaUrg) without touching the architecture or the training budget. The boolean primitive then failed the same way: its proxy asked whether a human agent was \emph{needed}, not whether the customer was \emph{asking} for anything, and the head trained on it scores \OldHeadAccNoul{} on the corrected question --- below the \MajAccNoul{} of a constant predictor --- while the same weights had looked strong on the proxy question. Re-grading the label with rules and retraining brings that primitive to \AccNoul{} (\BAccNoul{} balanced) against \RulesBAccNoul{} for the keyword rules. We do not, however, claim the concept is repaired: on \AntibodyN{} hand-written rows that separate the cue words from the meaning, that head's balanced accuracy is \AntibodyBAcc{} --- below the \num{0.5} of a constant predictor. Re-grading fixes the label; it only partly fixes what the model learns. Second, \emph{temperature is a joint property of weights and inference backend}: the identical fitting procedure returns \TChoice/\TScore/\TNoul{} on the training graph but \TChoiceOnnx/\TScoreOnnx/\TNoulOnnx{} on the quantised graph, so we treat calibration as a per-variant artefact that must ship with the model. We report every number through a script-generated table so that reviewers can regenerate the paper from the repository.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Ticket triage is a high-volume, low-tolerance decision problem. A support queue must decide, for every incoming ticket, who handles it (\texttt{choice} over five teams), how fast (\texttt{score} over five urgency levels), and whether the ticket carries refund-or-escalation risk (\texttt{noul}). Getting this wrong is expensive in both directions: routing a refund complaint to a hardware team wastes a scarce human hour, while missing an explicit escalation demand is a service-level failure.

Recent work has shown that a single encoder can answer multiple structured decision primitives in one forward pass without generating tokens, and that such decision heads can be trained with a reinforcement-learning objective that compares the log-probabilities of the available options rather than of generated text~\cite{laya}. That family of models, which we call System~One decision models, is attractive here because it replaces autoregressive generation with a bounded classification-style computation: latency is predictable, the output space is closed, and the model can be calibrated.

This paper is an engineering-and-evaluation report on \textbf{mai-jev}, an Apache-2.0 Chinese-first model in that family, built and shipped end-to-end: data audit, fine-tuning, temperature calibration, quantised export, CPU latency benchmarking, and reproduction scripts. The system is deliberately sized for a low-end deployment target --- an x86 laptop without a GPU, four threads, at most \SI{1}{\giga\byte} resident memory --- which constrains the model to a few hundred million parameters and rules out every distillation-to-a-teacher pipeline.

Our contributions are threefold.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A working Chinese System~One triage model.} A \DataTotalRows-row Chinese-primary dataset with audited provenance, an RLCD fine-tune of the 322M multilingual base, and three-primitive accuracy of \MacroAcc{} macro-average against \OrigAccMacro{} for the unmodified base and \RulesAccChoice/\RulesAccScore/\RulesAccNoul{} for a keyword rule system, at a worst-case ECE of \WorstEce{} after temperature scaling --- above our \num{0.10} target, reported as a miss. For the boolean primitive, where re-grading flipped the majority class, we judge on balanced accuracy instead: \BAccNoul{} against \RulesBAccNoul{} for the keyword rules and \MajAccNoul{} for the constant predictor.
  \item \textbf{A quantified account of proxy-label damage, hit twice.} We show that a semantically wrong label for one primitive cost \AccScoreVZeroTwo{} against \AccScore{} on that primitive, that more training epochs do not recover it, and that a purely rule-based evidence grader with no external API restores it. We then show the same failure on the boolean primitive: the head trained on the ``human needed'' proxy scores \OldHeadAccNoul{} on the honest question, below the constant baseline, and rule-based re-grading plus retraining raises it to \AccNoul{}/\BAccNoul{} --- but only on the natural distribution. We report the counterexample slice that keeps us honest: on \AntibodyN{} hand-written rows where the cue words and the meaning pull apart, the repaired head is at \AntibodyBAcc{} balanced, below a constant predictor. The synthetic labels share an origin with the rule baseline, so the measured scores still measure rule reproduction and generalisation, not ground-truth business correctness.
  \item \textbf{Deployment discipline for calibrated models.} We show that a whole-graph int8 dynamic quantisation is unusable for this encoder (macro accuracy collapses), that quantising only the embedding \texttt{Gather} nodes preserves accuracy at \ModelMb\,MB, and that each quantisation variant must re-fit its own temperatures. We also report a padding-dependent numerical bug that constrains inference to batch size one, and the measurement that latency on our hardware is dominated by the question-head token budget rather than by the ticket text.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{System~One decision models.} Laya~\cite{laya} answers choice, score and noul primitives over a shared encoder with per-primitive heads, trained with a reinforcement-learning decision objective. We adopt its checkpoint and objective unchanged; our contribution is the data, the calibration, and the deployment packaging, not the architecture. Training-free variants such as SemIf~\cite{semif} read calibrated logits out of an unmodified language model; we reject that route here because it produces no artefact to calibrate, quantise, or publish, and because our measured baseline numbers for the original checkpoint are substantially below what a fine-tune reaches (\OrigAccMacro{} macro).

\paragraph{Optimisation and calibration.} We fine-tune with an RLCD objective in the group-relative family of GRPO~\cite{grpo}, and we calibrate with per-primitive temperature scaling~\cite{calibrated}, which preserves the argmax and therefore cannot trade accuracy for calibration. This is a deliberate choice: in our experiments every post-calibration accuracy equals its pre-calibration value (\MacroAccBefore{} before, \MacroAcc{} after), and all of the calibration gain shows up in ECE.

\paragraph{Efficient inference.} The encoder is a ModernBERT-style bidirectional encoder~\cite{modernbert}; the 256\,000-entry embedding table dominates the parameter count. ONNX Runtime~\cite{onnxruntime} offers dynamic post-training quantisation~\cite{dynamicquant}, which we evaluate in detail in Section~\ref{sec:quant} and find necessary but insufficient: quantising every \texttt{MatMul} is what breaks it.

\paragraph{Data.} Our Chinese sources are an open Taiwanese customer-service corpus and an open multimodal-reasoning decision corpus (MIT), plus a small held-out evaluation-only Chinese decision benchmark (CC~BY~4.0). We audit licences and provenance in \texttt{DATASETS.md} and train only on sources whose licence permits it.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Task and data}

A ticket is a Chinese text \emph{state}. Three questions are asked of it, defined once in \texttt{mai\_jev/questions.py} and reused verbatim for training, calibration, export and inference:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{route\_team} (\texttt{choice}, 5 options): 售后 / 退款处理 / 技术支持 / 账务 / 投诉升级.
  \item \texttt{urgency} (\texttt{score}, 5 levels, index 0 lowest): from 完全不紧急 to 非常紧急.
  \item \texttt{needs\_refund\_or\_escalation} (\texttt{noul}): false/true.
\end{itemize}

The dataset is built by \texttt{scripts/build\_dataset.py} from audited raw sources into a fixed-seed split of \RowsTrain{} train, \RowsCalib{} calibration and \RowsTest{} test rows (\DataTotalRows{} total, seed \SeedAll{}). The calibration split is disjoint from training and is never trained on. Token statistics are reported against a \TokBudget{}-token budget, with p50 \TokPFiveZero, p99 \TokPNineNine and max \TokMax, and zero rows over budget.

\subsection{The proxy-label problem}

The first end-to-end fine-tune (\texttt{v02}) produced a model whose routing accuracy was excellent and whose urgency accuracy was \AccScoreVZeroTwo{} --- far below the keyword rule baseline \RulesAccScore{}. The cause was in the data, not the model: the only Chinese corpus with urgency-like labels annotated \emph{emotion intensity} (how upset is the customer), which is a different latent variable from ticket urgency (how fast must a human act). No amount of extra epochs helps, because the objective is being asked to fit the wrong function; we verified this by running longer schedules with no improvement.

We therefore wrote \texttt{scripts/label\_urgency.py}, a graded-evidence labeler that assigns one of five levels from accumulated positive and negative evidence in the ticket text, with conflicting evidence resolving to the higher level. It is deliberately stronger than the keyword rules of our rule baseline: it accumulates multiple evidence classes, cancels negative evidence, and handles the common case of a refund request with no time signal as level~1 rather than level~4. Crucially it overwrites \emph{only} the urgency dimension --- the route labels from the audited sources are preserved --- and it calls no external API, which keeps the pipeline reproducible and avoids distilling another model's mistakes. (Leaving the boolean label alone here, as we did, turned out to be the same mistake one level down; Section~\ref{sec:noul} re-grades it.)

The resulting label distribution is far from uniform and highly skewed toward the low end (\UrgDistAfterZero/\UrgDistAfterOne/\UrgDistAfterTwo/\UrgDistAfterThree/\UrgDistAfterFour{} rows across the five test levels). \texttt{scripts/balance\_urgency.py} resamples the training split to \RowsTrainBalanced{} rows with each urgency level equally represented. The urgency numbers below come from this \texttt{v03} checkpoint.

\subsection{The second proxy label}
\label{sec:noul}

The boolean primitive carried the same defect as urgency, one level down. Its labels come from the source corpora's ``does a human agent need to intervene'' annotation, while the question we actually ask is whether the customer is requesting a refund, compensation or escalation. In every split the proxy is positive roughly two thirds of the time (\NoulPosBefore{} on train, \NoulPosTestBefore{} on test), and most positives contain no refund-or-escalation cue at all --- they are simply emotional or time-pressured tickets. A head trained on that signal inherits a second emotion detector.

We re-graded the boolean label with \texttt{scripts/label\_noul.py}, a rule-based evidence ladder with no external API, under an explicit operational definition: \emph{true} for an explicit demand (``refund the money to my card''), for an escalation threat (``if this is not fixed I will report you''), and for an eligibility or possibility question about a refund, compensation or escalation (``can I still return this?''); \emph{false} for questions about process, rules or policy (``how do I file a complaint?''), for tickets that only express emotion or urgency, and for customers who explicitly deny having a request (``nothing is wrong, I am just curious whether refunds exist''). The distinction between asking about \emph{eligibility} and asking about \emph{process} is the part that a keyword table cannot make, so the labeler is gated on a hand-written counterexample set: \NoulAntibodyN{} rows on which the cue words and the correct verdict deliberately disagree. The labeler must reproduce every one of them or the script refuses to emit data, and the keyword rule baseline of our comparison answers \NoulGateDisagree{} of them differently from the rubric --- which is the point: the rubric is not the keyword table it is measured against.

Re-grading drops the positive rate from \NoulPosBefore{} to \NoulPosTrainAfter{} on train and \NoulPosTestAfter{} on test, so the majority class rises to \NoulMajTestAfter{}: from here on, accuracy on this primitive mostly measures the prior. We do not oversample or re-balance the boolean primitive --- only urgency is balanced --- precisely so that the evaluation stays on the natural distribution, and we judge the primitive on balanced accuracy. We then retrained with hyperparameters identical to \texttt{v03} (same seed, schedule, batch size and learning rates; the training files are row-for-row identical apart from the boolean label), giving the \texttt{v04} checkpoint that every remaining number in this paper describes. Scoring the \emph{old} head against the corrected question gives \OldHeadAccNoul{} accuracy and \OldHeadBAccNoul{} balanced --- below the \MajAccNoul{} constant baseline --- which is the clearest statement of what the proxy label had bought us.

\subsection{Training}

We fine-tune the multilingual base with Laya's RLCD loss~\cite{laya}: perturbed log-probabilities over the option set, a group-relative advantage, and a soft cross-entropy anchor, over two epochs with effective batch 32 and a fixed seed. Training takes about fifteen minutes on a single consumer GPU. The model is evaluated on the disjoint test split with three baselines: the unmodified base checkpoint, a keyword rule system, and the majority-class predictor.

\subsection{Temperature calibration}

Each primitive gets its own temperature fitted on the \RowsCalib{}-row calibration split by minimising negative log-likelihood, and ECE is measured with 15 equal-width bins on the test split. Temperature scaling cannot change an argmax, which is why Section~\ref{sec:results} reports identical accuracy before and after calibration. The three fitted temperatures differ by an order of magnitude --- \TChoice, \TScore, \TNoul{} --- with the score primitive needing the largest correction, i.e.\ it was the most overconfident before fitting.

\subsection{Deployment packaging}

\texttt{scripts/build\_export.py} assembles a self-contained directory: the ONNX graph, a \texttt{calibration.json} fitted on that graph, \texttt{questions.json}, a \texttt{deploy\_config.json}, the tokenizer, and a \texttt{MANIFEST.json} with per-file SHA-256. \texttt{mai\_jev/deploy.py} is the inference path: \texttt{onnxruntime} plus \texttt{tokenizers} plus \texttt{numpy}, with no PyTorch and no Transformers. It renders each question head exactly as the training code does, records the marker positions of each option inside the sequence, and runs one ticket at a time at exact length (Section~\ref{sec:quant} explains why).

One design rule is worth stating as a general lesson. The head budget must be a \emph{build-time invariant}: an earlier build reused a stale sequence-length setting, the state silently lost its budget entirely, and routing accuracy collapsed from \AccChoice{} to \AccCollapsed{} while producing perfectly well-formed outputs. Both the exporter and the deployer now fail loudly if the head does not fit. The general failure mode is that a head budget error is invisible in the output schema --- it degrades the model into reading only the question --- which is why we added an explicit check rather than relying on the loss curve.

\subsection{Latency is head-bound, not text-bound}

The p50 above is several times what one might expect from a 322M model on four threads, and the reason is not the encoder. The question heads are long --- the \texttt{choice} and \texttt{score} criteria are one sentence of Chinese per option --- so the rendered heads occupy \HeadLenChoice{} and \HeadLenScore{} tokens, against a median ticket body of \TokPFiveZero{} tokens at training length, out of \BenchMaxLen{} allowed. Inside one session, the three heads with \emph{no} ticket text at all already cost \StateOnlyMs\,ms, against \StateFullMs\,ms for a real ticket read in full: most of the bill is the question, not the document, which is why shortening the document cannot bring a 322M encoder to a per-question budget of \num{50}~ms. (Absolute figures move with the host as in Table~\ref{tab:eff}; the comparison is within one session.) Two consequences follow, and both are negative results:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A per-question p50 target below \num{50}~ms is unreachable for this class of model on four CPU threads; the budget is consumed by the question, not the document. Meeting such a target requires shortening the criteria themselves, which changes the model's semantics, or a smaller encoder.
  \item We tested batching the three primitives into one forward pass. It is \emph{slower} (\BatchMergedMs\,ms versus \BatchSeparateMs\,ms for three batch-one calls) because the encoder runs per row regardless, and the three questions produce genuinely different sequences.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Quantisation, calibration and a padding bug}

\label{sec:quant}

Whole-graph dynamic int8 quantisation, the obvious choice, fails on this encoder: accuracy collapses to roughly a quarter of fp32 and the logit error is large on the score primitive. The reason is that ONNX Runtime's dynamic quantiser touches every \texttt{MatMul}, and a bidirectional encoder's dynamic range does not survive per-channel int8. Quantising only the \texttt{Gather} nodes --- the embedding table, which is about half the graph --- and leaving \texttt{MatMul} in fp32 gives \ModelMb\,MB with a maximum absolute logit deviation of about \num{0.1} and unchanged argmax decisions on all three primitives. Static QDQ and QOperator quantisation were also attempted and rejected for toolchain failures; int4 is unsupported by the available quantiser.

Two deployment facts follow from this and are worth stating separately, because both are silent failure modes.

\paragraph{Temperature is per-variant.} Fitting temperatures on the quantised graph --- with the same procedure, on the same calibration split --- gives \TChoiceOnnx/\TScoreOnnx/\TNoulOnnx{} instead of \TChoice/\TScore/\TNoul{}. This is not a bug in the fitter: quantisation changes the logit scale, so the confidence that the weights imply is not the confidence the graph emits. Reusing a temperature across variants pushes the worst per-primitive ECE above the \num{0.10} target; per-variant fitting lowers it to \EceWorstOnnx{}, which still misses the line. We report that miss, together with the training graph's, rather than choose a temperature on the test split. We therefore ship a separate \texttt{calibration.json} inside every export directory and treat cross-variant reuse as a release error.

\paragraph{Batching breaks numerics.} With right-padded batches containing more than one row, short \texttt{noul} rows return non-finite scores, while the same rows are correct when run alone and when run eagerly in PyTorch. The symptom is confined to the score/noul head path under padding in the exported graph. Because batch size one is also the shape our latency target requires, we deploy batch one and document the constraint rather than working around a graph we cannot yet explain.

\section{Limitations, negative results and ethics}

\paragraph{The urgency accuracy is not business accuracy.} Our urgency labels are produced by an evidence grader we wrote. That grader is the same family of artefact as our rule baseline, so the model's \AccScore{} on the urgency primitive measures reproduction-plus-generalisation of that grader, not correctness against human urgency judgements. Two independent signals support caution: the balanced accuracy on this primitive is only \BAccScore{}, well below the \BAccChoice{} of routing, and a manual spot-check of \SelfCheckN{} sampled tickets found \SelfCheckDisagree{} cases where the model and the rule baseline disagree, which is the expected signature of a model that has generalised rather than memorised. A human-labelled urgency set is the missing ingredient, and we regard it as the highest-value next step rather than a footnote.

\paragraph{Calibration margins are thin, and on this checkpoint the target is missed.} The worst per-primitive ECE after fitting is \WorstEce{}, against our \num{0.10} target. The cause is identifiable and, we think, more useful than the miss: on the routing primitive the likelihood-fitted temperature \emph{raises} test ECE from \EceBeforeChoice{} to \EceChoice{}, because the \RowsCalib-row calibration split's accuracy differs from the test split's by more than the confidence gap; the previous checkpoint behaved the same way at almost the same temperature, so at \RowsTest{} rows the ``below \num{0.10}'' line is decided by sampling noise rather than by method. We did not select the temperature by test ECE --- that would be fitting on the test set --- and we report the miss. Fixing it properly means a larger calibration split, or choosing the temperature by ECE on a held-out part of it: a change to the calibration method, not to this model. We therefore do not claim that the model is ``reliably calibrated'' in general; we claim that on this held-out Chinese ticket distribution, at this model size, after per-variant fitting, the measured worst-case ECE is \WorstEce{}. Temperature fitting minimises likelihood, not ECE, and the direction of the ECE change per primitive is not uniform, which is visible in Figure~\ref{fig:ece}. The quantised variant we ship misses the same line in the same direction (\EceWorstOnnx{}), so the miss is a property of this checkpoint at this size, not of the export path.

\paragraph{The second proxy label is repaired in the data, and only partly in the model.} Section~\ref{sec:noul} re-grades the boolean label and retrains, so that label debt is closed: the primitive reaches \AccNoul{} accuracy and \BAccNoul{} balanced, against \MajAccNoul{} and \RulesBAccNoul{} for the constant and keyword baselines. What is \emph{not} closed is what the model learned. On the \AntibodyN-row counterexample set --- hand-written, present in no split, and built so that the cue words contradict the verdict --- the same head reaches \AntibodyAcc{} accuracy and \AntibodyBAcc{} balanced, below the \AntibodyMajAcc{} of always answering ``no''. The keyword rules do worse still (\AntibodyRulesBAcc{} balanced), so the head is not merely a keyword table; but with \RowsTrain{} training rows it reproduces the new label distribution without acquiring the boundary cases that define it. Feeding such counterexamples into training, or building a human-audited boolean set, is the next step; until then we treat this primitive as review-only rather than automatable.

\paragraph{Hardware.} All latency and memory numbers were measured on a desktop-class x86 CPU with four threads. These figures move by more than anything the model does --- the identical binary spanned \LatPFiveZeroBest{} to \LatPFiveZeroWorst\,ms p50 across \LatSessions{} sessions on that host --- and the deployment target is an older machine, which can only be slower. Our numbers are therefore one session's optimistic bound, not a demonstration of the target's capability.

\paragraph{Data licences and use.} Training data is drawn from MIT-licensed and CC~BY~4.0 sources with attribution recorded in \texttt{DATASETS.md}; CC~BY~4.0 attribution obligations do not extend into model weights, but we retain the notices regardless. The Chinese decision benchmark is used for held-out evaluation only and was never trained on; the model card states this. The model card also records that no proprietary or paid annotation API was used to create any training label, since distilling a teacher's mistakes into a calibration target is worse than having no teacher.

\paragraph{Scope.} mai-jev is a weights-and-scripts release. There is no hosted inference service, no multimodal input, and no mobile (arm64) build: we had no arm64 device to validate against, and the int4 toolchain our target would need is unavailable.

\section{Reproducibility}

Every quantitative statement in this paper resolves to a macro in \texttt{paper/generated/numbers.tex}, and every macro is generated by \texttt{scripts/make\_paper\_tables.py} from JSON artefacts produced by the training, calibration, export and benchmark scripts. No number in the text is transcribed by hand; if an input artefact is missing the generator exits with an error rather than emitting a stale value. Both label graders carry their own gate: \texttt{scripts/label\_urgency.py} and \texttt{scripts/label\_noul.py} refuse to emit data when a hand-written counterexample set contradicts them, and \texttt{scripts/eval\_noul\_antibody.py} reproduces the counterexample-slice numbers of Section~\ref{sec:noul} from a shipped checkpoint. The repository carries the full chain --- dataset construction, RLCD training, temperature fitting, ONNX export and quantisation, packaging, CPU benchmarking, and evaluation of the exported artefact --- plus the data provenance and licence audit. The base checkpoint is Apache-2.0, and mai-jev is released under the same licence as a modified version, retaining upstream attribution.
